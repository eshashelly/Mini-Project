Visualization Descriptions and Insights
1. Total Electric Vehicles (KPI Card)

This KPI card represents the total electric range contribution of all electric vehicles in the dataset, which is approximately 11 million. This value indicates the overall scale of electric vehicle performance captured in the dataset. The large total suggests that electric vehicles have become increasingly common and contribute significantly to sustainable transportation systems. This metric provides a quick overview of the overall EV capacity and highlights the growing importance of electric mobility in the automotive industry.

2. Average Electric Range (KPI Card)

The Average Electric Range KPI displays an average value of 39.44, which represents the typical distance an electric vehicle can travel on a full charge based on the dataset. This metric reflects advancements in battery technology and energy efficiency. A higher average range increases consumer confidence in EV adoption by reducing range anxiety and making electric vehicles more practical for everyday transportation.

3. Number of Cities with EVs (KPI Card)

This KPI shows that electric vehicles appear across 276.83K city records, indicating the broad geographic spread of EV adoption. The presence of EVs in such a large number of locations highlights the expansion of EV infrastructure and growing awareness of environmentally friendly transportation solutions. This also suggests that EV adoption is no longer limited to a few urban centers but is spreading across multiple regions.

4. Electric Vehicle Growth Over Time (Line Chart)

The line chart illustrates the growth of electric vehicles based on model year. The chart shows very minimal EV presence before 2010, followed by gradual growth until around 2015. After 2015, there is a sharp increase in EV production and adoption, reaching a peak around 2022. This trend reflects the increasing global demand for electric vehicles driven by factors such as government incentives, environmental regulations, technological advancements, and rising fuel costs. The rapid growth pattern demonstrates how EVs are becoming a mainstream alternative to traditional vehicles.

5. Electric Vehicle Type Share (Donut Chart)

The donut chart visualizes the market share between Battery Electric Vehicles (BEVs) and Plug-in Hybrid Electric Vehicles (PHEVs). The data indicates that BEVs account for approximately 79.95% of the total EV market, while PHEVs represent around 20.05%. This significant difference highlights a strong market preference for fully electric vehicles. Consumers are increasingly choosing BEVs due to improvements in charging infrastructure, longer driving ranges, and growing environmental awareness.

6. EV Distribution by City (Map Visualization)

The map visualization presents the geographic distribution of electric vehicles across different cities around the world. The visual shows clusters of EV adoption mainly in North America and parts of Europe, which suggests that developed regions with strong infrastructure and supportive government policies tend to adopt EV technology faster. This visualization helps identify areas with high EV penetration and regions where future market expansion opportunities may exist.

7. Electric Vehicles by Make (Bar Chart)

The bar chart compares the number of electric vehicles produced by different manufacturers. It clearly shows that Tesla has the highest number of EVs, significantly outperforming other companies. Other notable manufacturers include Chevrolet, Nissan, Ford, Kia, Toyota, BMW, Hyundai, and Rivian. Tesla’s leading position highlights its strong influence and innovation in the EV industry, while the presence of other manufacturers indicates increasing competition in the electric vehicle market.

8. EV Type by Model Year (Stacked Bar Chart)

This visualization displays the distribution of EV types (BEV and PHEV) across different model years. The chart shows that earlier years had relatively fewer electric vehicles available. However, in more recent years, there has been a noticeable increase in Battery Electric Vehicles compared to Plug-in Hybrid Electric Vehicles. This suggests that manufacturers are gradually shifting their focus toward fully electric vehicles rather than hybrid technologies, reflecting future industry trends.

9. Electric Vehicle Details Table

The table provides detailed information about individual electric vehicle models, including the manufacturer, model name, model year, electric range, and EV type. It allows users to examine specific vehicle characteristics and compare different EV models. Popular models such as Tesla Model Y, Tesla Model 3, Nissan Leaf, and Chevrolet Bolt EV appear in the dataset, demonstrating their importance in driving EV market growth.

10. EV Model Performance and Range Comparison

The dataset also highlights variations in electric range among different vehicle models, showing how performance differs between manufacturers and vehicle types. Some vehicles offer significantly higher ranges, which indicates ongoing improvements in battery capacity and efficiency. These advancements are critical for increasing consumer acceptance of electric vehicles and reducing concerns related to charging limitations.

11. Slicers (Interactive Filters)

The dashboard includes slicers for Electric Vehicle Type, allowing users to filter the entire report based on specific categories such as Battery Electric Vehicles (BEV) and Plug-in Hybrid Electric Vehicles (PHEV).

These slicers improve the interactivity and usability of the dashboard by enabling users to dynamically explore the dataset and analyze trends for a specific vehicle type. When a slicer option is selected, all related visualizations in the dashboard automatically update to display filtered results.
